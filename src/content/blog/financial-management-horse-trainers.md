# Running the Numbers: Financial Management in TrainingTree Pro and StableTree

Barns run on thin margins and a lot of small charges: board, training days, farrier and vet pass-throughs, supplements, extra turnout, shipping. Keeping track of them by hand is slow, and things are easy to miss.

TrainingTree Pro and StableTree include a complete financial system built around horses and their owners. This article walks through what it does.

## Proper double-entry books

At the core is a **double-entry ledger** with a full chart of accounts, the same accounting structure your accountant works with.

- Income, expense, asset, liability, equity and transfer transactions.
- Multiple currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, CAD, AUD and JPY).
- Common payment methods recorded against each transaction: cash, check, bank transfer, ACH, wire and card.
- **Reversals done properly.** Reversing a posted transaction writes a new, opposing entry and leaves the original in place, so the audit trail stays intact.

## Invoicing owners

- **Line items with tax and discounts,** worked out by the system so every invoice adds up. Discounts come off before tax.
- **Recurring invoices** for board and training fees.
- **Partial payments** that carry the remaining balance forward.
- **An invoice lifecycle** from draft to sent, viewed, paid, overdue, cancelled or refunded.
- **Overdue reminders** on a 3, 7 and 14-day schedule, once email is configured for the barn.

## Shared ownership and commissions

Many horses have more than one owner. The system handles:

- **Ownership by percentage shares,** with multiple partners per horse.
- **Revenue distribution** by type: race winnings, breeding fees, sale proceeds and lease income.
- **Owner split-billing,** so each partner is invoiced their share.
- **Trainer commissions** with a per-horse rate and any number of recipients, as a percentage or a flat amount.

## Knowing your costs

### Per horse
Each horse carries its own ledger of income and expenses, so you can see what it costs to keep and what it brings in.

### Labour
The time-clock moves with the job. When a staff member finishes one task and starts the next, the time is booked to the right horse and task. Labour can then be broken down by horse, task, day and employee. Pay rates are visible only to people you give permission to.

### Suppliers
The **vendor price view** tracks each supplier's monthly spend and flags whether it's rising, falling or steady, which is useful when a hay or bedding price creeps up.

### Receipts
Staff can photograph a receipt on a phone and attach it to the expense.

## Budgets and reports

- **Budgets** with budget-versus-actual tracking.
- **Income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow reports,** exportable to CSV, Excel or PDF.
- **A straight-line forecast** of revenue and expenses once there are at least three months of posted activity. It's a simple trend line and it's labelled as one.

## Working with your accountant

- **QuickBooks and Xero export.** The ledger exports as a QuickBooks Desktop IIF file or a general-journal CSV for Xero. Each transaction becomes a balanced entry, and anything that can't be exported cleanly is listed for you to fix.
- **Your data stays yours.** A full barn export downloads everything the barn has recorded, one file per table.

## Owners see the care behind the bill

Through the **owner portal**, each owner sees what the barn chooses to share for their horse: health, care, work and invoices. Owners can send requests such as extra turnout, grooming, the farrier or the vet directly to the barn's daily list. Invoices arrive with the services behind them.

## Getting set up

1. **Set up the chart of accounts** with your accountant, or start from the default.
2. **Add owners and ownership shares** for each horse.
3. **Create recurring invoices** for board and training fees.
4. **Start logging services and expenses** against each horse as they happen.
5. **Export to your accounting software** at month end.
