# Our Approach to Horse Wellbeing: The Five Domains, Built Into the Barn

Ask ten horse people whether a horse is doing well and you'll get ten good answers, each built on a different part of the picture. The groom knows he cleaned up his feed. The manager knows he went out with his friends. The vet knows his vaccinations are current. The trainer knows he's had two easy days. Nobody holds the whole picture at once.

TrainingTree Pro does. Its **Wellbeing Check** pulls together the records a barn already keeps and lays them out on one of the most widely used welfare frameworks in animal science, so everyone around the horse sees the same picture.

## Why the Five Domains

The Five Domains model, developed by David Mellor and colleagues and updated in 2020, is how welfare scientists organise the question "how is this animal doing?" It looks at four physical and functional areas, then at the fifth, the animal's mental state, which is understood through the other four.

We built the Wellbeing Check on it for three reasons:

- **It's published and open.** Anyone can read *The 2020 Five Domains Model* (Mellor et al., *Animals* 10(10):1870) and see exactly what each domain means.
- **It's about the horse's experience,** not just whether the paperwork is up to date.
- **It's how welfare is increasingly judged** by vets, competition bodies and the public.

## What the check looks at

Each part comes from records that already exist in the barn's day-to-day work in TrainingTree Pro.

**Nutrition**
- **Body condition:** the latest Henneke score. The ideal range is 4 to 6.
- **Feeding:** whether feeding is being logged day to day.

**Physical environment**
- **Turnout:** how often the horse has been going out.

**Health**
- **Vaccinations:** whether any are past their next-due date.
- **Deworming:** whether the next dose is overdue.
- **Hoof care:** whether the farrier visit is overdue.
- **Injuries:** any active injury, scored by its severity.
- **Workload:** whether the horse's work is building steadily or rising faster than it has been prepared for.

**Behavioural interactions**
- **Company:** turnout with other horses.
- **Rest:** regular days without work.

**Mental state**
- Inferred from the four domains above, exactly as the model describes. It's never given a number of its own, because records can't measure what a horse feels. They can only show the conditions that shape it.

## Designed to be fair to every barn

Barns keep records differently. One logs every feed; another tracks turnout on a whiteboard and feeding in someone's head. A wellbeing tool that punished a horse for what the barn didn't type in would be measuring the software, not the horse.

So the Wellbeing Check follows three rules:

1. **A part with no records is left out, never scored as zero.** If your barn doesn't log turnout in TrainingTree Pro, the turnout part simply doesn't appear.
2. **Every domain counts equally.** A domain with five records can't drown out a domain with one. Weighting one area of a horse's life over another is a welfare judgement we leave to you and your vet.
3. **The overall result needs enough evidence.** With fewer than three scored parts, or fewer than two scored domains, the check tells you what it needs instead of producing a number.

Every part shows the record behind it ("Turned out on 5 of the last 7 days", "Henneke 5 on 14 October, ideal 4–6"), so you can see what each result is based on.

## What it's for

The Wellbeing Check is a records-based view of a horse's welfare. It doesn't replace your vet or a behavioural assessment, and it says so on screen. What it does is make the everyday parts of good care visible:

- **For barn managers,** a horse whose turnout has quietly dropped or whose farrier date has slipped shows up before anyone has to notice.
- **For trainers,** workload and rest sit right next to body condition and injuries, so you see the whole athlete.
- **For owners,** the care you pay for becomes something you can see.
- **For vets,** the history between visits is already organised.

## Part of a bigger picture

Wellbeing in TrainingTree Pro doesn't stop at the check. It runs through the whole platform:

- **Vitals against each horse's own normal.** Temperature and resting pulse are compared with that horse's own normal, so a fever shows up even when the reading looks "normal" in a textbook.
- **Rehab plans that hold the line.** While a plan is active, every training suggestion stays inside its stage.
- **Feed that follows the work,** set from the horse's logged workload using the National Research Council's *Nutrient Requirements of Horses* (2007).

Good horse care has always depended on people who notice. TrainingTree Pro gives everyone around the horse the same view, every morning.
