# Feed That Follows the Work

A horse in light work and the same horse three weeks into a serious build need different rations. Most feed charts don't know the difference. They're written once and changed when someone remembers to.

TrainingTree sets nutrition from the work the horse has **actually done**, using the standard reference in equine nutrition.

## Built on NRC 2007

The National Research Council's *Nutrient Requirements of Horses* (6th revised edition, 2007) sets out what horses need by body weight and work level. TrainingTree uses its tables for:

- **Digestible energy**
- **Crude protein and lysine**
- **Calcium and phosphorus**
- **Sodium**
- **Vitamin E and selenium**

## Work level from the work itself

Instead of asking you to pick "light", "moderate" or "heavy" and hoping it stays true, TrainingTree reads the horse's **recent logged sessions** and works out the NRC work category from them. Step up the training and the requirements follow. Give the horse a quiet month and they ease back.

## Timing around hard work

Hard sessions change what a horse needs and when. TrainingTree notices:

- **After a hard session,** the horse is in a recovery phase, and guidance focuses on refuelling.
- **Before a hard session** scheduled today or tomorrow, guidance focuses on fuelling.

Each piece of guidance says when it applies (before, after or daily), what it is and why, with its source.

## A ration that fits the gut

The ration sketch keeps to well-established limits for digestive health:

- **Forage first:** hay at 1.5–2% of body weight.
- **Starch limits:** no more than 1 g per kg of body weight per meal, and 2 g per kg per day.
- **Oil** up to 1 mL per kg where extra energy is needed.

Any assumption the ration relies on, such as the energy content of a feed, is written next to it.

## What the horse thinks

Horses have opinions about feed. TrainingTree reads the barn's feeding records from the last 60 days and **flags feeds a horse keeps refusing or leaving**, along with any allergies or restrictions in its diet plan. The ration you plan is one the horse will actually eat.

## Supplements, without the guesswork

Supplement suggestions stay with what the evidence supports for the work level: salt for every horse, electrolytes once work is moderate or harder, and vitamin E for horses in heavy work.

Feed planning is part of TrainingTree and StableTree, alongside body-condition scoring and the Wellbeing Check, so nutrition, condition and welfare stay in one view.
