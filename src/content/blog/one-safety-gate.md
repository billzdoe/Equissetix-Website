# One Safety Gate in Front of Every Workout

Most soft-tissue injuries in athletes don't come from hard work. They come from **hard work arriving too fast**: a big week after an easy month, a horse back from a break who feels fresh and gets asked for too much.

Sports science has a well-studied way to see that coming, and TrainingTree Pro puts it in front of every workout suggestion it makes.

## The workload ratio

The **acute-to-chronic workload ratio** compares what a horse has done lately with what it's used to. It was developed in human sport, where Hulin and colleagues (*British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2016) and Gabbett (2016) linked sharp rises in the ratio to a higher risk of injury, and steady, well-built chronic load to resilience.

- Around **0.8 to 1.3**, a horse is building fitness at a pace its body can absorb.
- **Above 1.3**, work is rising faster than the horse has prepared for.
- **Above 1.5**, the rise is steep.

TrainingTree Pro works the ratio out from every logged session using a fixed 28-day window, computed the same way in every chart, card and recommendation. The number on the horse's page is always the number the advice was based on.

## The gate

Every suggestion, from every part of TrainingTree Pro, passes through the same check before you see it:

1. **Is the workload ratio above 1.3?** Then the suggestion eases off.
2. **Is the horse's readiness red?** Then today is not the day to push.
3. **Is there an active rehab plan?** Then the work stays inside that plan's stage.

That's one gate, shared by every surface that suggests work: quick suggestions, the conditioning programs, the decision matrix and the training plans. A horse coming back from injury is never offered the same work as a sound one, wherever in the app the suggestion comes from.

## Readiness, honestly built

The **daily readiness score** (0–100) brings together four inputs: health, training load, recovery since the last hard session, and injury risk.

- **Green (80–100):** ready to push.
- **Yellow (50–79):** moderate work.
- **Red (0–49):** rest.

When some inputs haven't been measured, the score says which ones it used and which it left out, so you always know how much evidence is behind it.

## Programs that adapt, session by session

TrainingTree Pro's conditioning programs for base fitness, eventing, endurance, flat racing, show jumping and dressage follow the published structure of conditioning: a base of long, slow work before strength and speed, progressive overload, and a lighter week every few weeks (Clayton 1991; Marlin & Nankervis 2002).

The difference is that each session is fitted to the horse it's for. When a horse's ratio is high, readiness is red or a rehab plan is active, the session changes, and **every change is listed with its reason**.
